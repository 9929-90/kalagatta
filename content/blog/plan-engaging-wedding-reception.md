## Begin with the guest experience

An engaging reception is not about adding too many activities. It is about making guests feel included while respecting their comfort. Plan what they will see, hear, and understand from arrival to dinner.

## Create a simple flow

A clean reception flow may include welcome, couple entry, family welcome, cake or ring moment if relevant, speeches, games, photography cues, dinner opening, and closing thanks. Keep the sequence practical.

## Make entries meaningful

Entries should match the couple's personality. Some couples want grand energy. Others prefer elegance. The anchor should introduce the moment in a way that fits the mood.

## Keep speeches short

Long speeches can slow the evening. Choose speakers early and give them time guidance. The anchor can introduce speakers warmly and transition quickly after each one.

## Use games carefully

One or two well-chosen games can bring energy. Avoid complicated rules or jokes that embarrass guests. Keep games inclusive and short.

## Protect dinner timing

Guests remember food delays. Coordinate the dinner opening with venue and catering teams. The anchor should make clear announcements so guests move smoothly.
