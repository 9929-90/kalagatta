## Delays are normal

Events rarely run exactly on paper. A performer may be late, the couple may need photos, a speaker may be unavailable, or sound may need adjustment. The anchor's job is to keep the guest experience smooth while the team fixes the issue.

## Do not expose backstage problems

Guests do not need every technical detail. Instead of saying there is a problem, the anchor can redirect attention, thank guests, invite applause, or move to a flexible segment.

## Use short interactions

For wedding delays, quick family shoutouts, couple questions, song prompts, or side-vs-side energy can work. For corporate delays, a concise recap or audience acknowledgment may be better. For private parties, light games can help.

## Keep information clear

If dinner is opening, the couple is arriving, or guests need to move spaces, say it clearly. Confusion makes delays feel longer.

## Coordinate before speaking

The anchor should check with planner, DJ, sound team, or family contact before changing the flow. Random stage decisions can create bigger problems.

## Prepare flexible segments

Good anchors keep a few flexible moments ready: quick games, applause prompts, acknowledgments, sponsor mentions, or photo cues. These segments can expand or shrink based on time.
