## Start with names and relationships

The strongest wedding scripts begin with accurate names and relationships. Collect couple names, parents, siblings, close friends, and special guests. Pronunciation matters.

## Welcome lines

A welcome should be warm and brief. It can mention the families, the city, the occasion, and the emotion of the day. Avoid overused lines that do not connect to the couple.

## Couple entry

Build anticipation before the couple enters. Mention their journey, the energy of the room, and the importance of the moment. Keep it grand but not too long.

## Performance introductions

Before each performance, say who is performing and why it matters. A cousin dance, parent act, or friends' performance becomes more meaningful with context.

## Game transitions

Explain rules clearly. Invite participants confidently. Keep the tone playful and family-safe. End the game with applause and a simple transition.

## Closing lines

Close with gratitude. Thank families, guests, vendors, and the couple. Direct guests to dinner, photography, DJ, or the next segment.
